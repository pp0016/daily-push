## The 7 Real Problems You'd Face

### Problem 1: You're Watching Survivorship Bias In Real-Time

You saw **one channel** (1221Recap) making $5K/month. You didn't see:
- The dozens of manhwa recap channels that never got monetized
- The ones that got DMCA'd and died
- The ones making $50/month with 100 videos
- Manhwa Outpost's 405K subs making only $1K/month

### Problem 2: A 5-Hour Video Is NOT 1-2 Hours Of Work

Even with full automation, a single 5-hour manhwa recap video requires:

| Task | Time (Automated) | Time (Manual) |
|------|------------------|---------------|
| Find a manhwa to cover | 15 min | 15 min |
| Source 200-400 panel images | 1-2 hours | 3-5 hours |
| Generate 50K-word script (AI) | 30 min generation + 1 hour review | — |
| Generate TTS audio (free tools) | 2-4 hours processing time | — |
| FFmpeg image pan/scroll + captions | 1-2 hours render | — |
| Quality check final video | 30 min (skim) | 5 hours (full) |
| Upload + metadata | 15 min | 15 min |
| **Total** | **~5-8 hours active work** | **12+ hours** |

Your 1-2 hours/day means one video takes 3-4 days minimum. At that rate, you'll produce 7-10 videos per month. 1221Recap does 5-6 per month with this format, so that's comparable — BUT you can't quality-check the scripts because you don't know the source material.

### Problem 3: Zero Manhwa Knowledge = Guaranteed Audience Rejection

I (AI) can generate a manhwa recap script. Here's what will go wrong:

- I'll mix up character names between similar manhwa (protagonist of "Solo Leveling" vs "Omniscient Reader" — different characters, similar tropes)
- I'll invent scenes that don't exist in the source material
- I'll get power system details wrong (levels, skills, ranks differ between every manhwa)
- I'll miss chapter-specific plot twists that the audience specifically wants to hear about
- You won't catch ANY of these errors because you've never read the source

**The comments section will eat you alive.** Manhwa readers are obsessive about accuracy.

### Problem 4: Copyright Strikes Are Not A Maybe — They're A When

The content uses manhwa panels directly. The publishers:
- **Webtoon** (owned by Naver) — actively issues DMCA takedowns
- **Tappytoon** — sends copyright claims
- **Kakao/Tapas** — automated content ID systems
- **Yen Press** — for licensed English translations

Three copyright strikes = channel permanently deleted. All your investment gone.

### Problem 5: YouTube's "Reused Content" Policy

YouTube's monetization review specifically flags:
> *"Content that's been uploaded many times by other creators, or text-to-speech narration over slideshow-style images"*

This is LITERALLY the format you're planning. 1221Recap got approved — but YouTube's review is inconsistent. Many channels in this exact format get rejected. You'd potentially invest 2-3 months of work before even knowing if you can monetize.

### Problem 6: Your Existing Channel Isn't Monetized

This is not an insult — it's diagnostic. If you haven't been able to grow an existing channel to 1K subs and 4K watch hours, starting a harder channel in a niche you don't understand won't fix the problem. The skills that get a channel to monetization (thumbnails, titles, consistency, audience understanding) are the same regardless of niche.

### Problem 7: You're Spread Too Thin

You said you have "lots of other ideas" and "other projects." Adding a manhwa channel that needs 1-2 hours/day on TOP of your existing channel and other projects means nothing gets your full attention. This is how you end up with 3 channels at 500 subs each instead of 1 channel at 10K subs.

## 🔴 MY RECOMMENDATION: DON'T DO THIS CHANNEL

### Pre-Mortem: The Two Most Likely Ways This Fails

**Failure Mode 1 (60% probability):** You invest 2-3 months, produce 15-20 videos, apply for monetization, and YouTube rejects you for "reused content." All work wasted. You can reapply, but the format doesn't change, so the result probably won't either.

**Failure Mode 2 (30% probability):** You get monetized, earn $500-1000/month for 2-3 months, then get a copyright strike from Webtoon/Tappytoon. Two more strikes and the channel is dead. Or the algorithm moves on (like it did to Manhwa Outpost), and your revenue drops to $100-200/month.

### Why I'm Saying No — Specific To YOUR Situation

| Your Constraint | Why It Kills This Idea |
|----------------|----------------------|
| Zero manhwa knowledge | Can't quality-check AI scripts. Audience will find errors in week 1. |
| 1-2 hours/day | One 5-hour video = 3-4 days of your time. Not enough output to compete. |
| Existing channel not monetized | You haven't proven you can grow ANY channel yet. This is a harder niche, not an easier one. |
| "AI can do everything" mindset | AI can generate, but can't quality-check against source material YOU haven't read. Garbage in, garbage out. |
| Multiple other projects | You'll abandon this in month 2 when the novelty wears off and you're still not monetized. |
| Chose niche based on one data point | You saw $3K/month on vidIQ for ONE channel. The 405K-sub channel in this niche makes $1K/month. |

### The Counter-Argument (Being Fair)

Yes, it IS possible to succeed. KAI Manhwa Recap hit 21K subs in 5 months. LY Manhwa Recap hit 8.5K subs in 3 months. The barrier to entry is low, and AI tools are getting better.

**But "possible" is not "probable" — especially for someone with zero domain knowledge, limited time, and an unproven track record.**

### What I'd Recommend Instead

If you want to earn from YouTube:

1. **Fix your existing channel first.** Get it to monetization. Learn what works — titles, thumbnails, retention, consistency. These skills transfer to ANY future channel.

2. **If you must start a new channel, pick a niche where:**
   - You have genuine knowledge (so you can quality-check AI output)
   - The content is original (not copyrighted material that can get DMCA'd)
   - The format is genuinely automatable in 1-2 hours/day
   - You're not competing against content farms

3. **If you absolutely insist on manhwa** — at minimum, read 2-3 popular manhwa first (Solo Leveling, Omniscient Reader, Beginning After The End). Spend 2 weeks reading before you produce a single video. Without this, your content will be detectably bad.
